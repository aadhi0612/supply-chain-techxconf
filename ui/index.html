AboutSiliconFlow

A reference implementation, built for TechXConf, of what supply planning for advanced packaging could look like when every fact has a source and every decision has an owner.

Chip supply chains fail in the gaps between systems: a supplier email in one inbox, a capacity change in a portal, an export rule in a spreadsheet. SiliconFlow joins them into one verified picture, shows what a disruption really costs, and carries the approved fix all the way back into the plan.

Deterministic first

Allocation is solved and independently re-verified. An LLM may only propose events from messy notices; it never decides anything physical, legal or irreversible.

Humans decide

Four-eyes approvals by role, policy-driven. The proposer cannot approve, and execution refuses a plan that has gone stale.

Evidence everywhere

Every fact carries source, time and confidence. Every step lands in a hash-chained audit log you can verify.

No customers yet: this is a working prototype on illustrative data, with honest limits documented in the architecture notes.

Whatwe do

Four jobs, one loop, from the first supplier signal to the supplier's confirmation.

1 · TrackTrackability

See every logic-die, HBM, substrate and module lot as a shipment between sites, and what that means for each customer order, week by week.

2 · TraceTraceability

Follow a module back to the lots and sites that made it, or a lot forward to every module and customer it reached. Design stage: the demo shows the idea, the engine does not store serial genealogy yet.

3 · RecoverImpact and options

Find the binding constraint, compare verified recovery options with best, base and worst outcomes, and get approvals from the right roles.

4 · ProveAudit

Keep a tamper-evident record of who proposed, approved and executed what, and which facts it rested on.

One incident,from signal toclosed loop

OSAT-A cuts reserved packaging 25% for weeks 4 to 9 and a substrate lot goes on quality hold. This is what happens next.

SignalNotice or structured event

The supplier notice becomes proposals with verbatim evidence. A planner confirms; a less authoritative source can worsen the plan at once but cannot improve it without confirmation.

ImpactIf nobody acts

1,276 on-time units lost across four orders, about $38.9M of exposure. The binding pools are packaging at OSAT-A and OSAT-B, found by relaxing each tight pool and re-solving.

OptionsSolver-backed, independently verified

Re-allocate only (8,900 on time), balanced recovery (about 9,432 on time for roughly $0.9M with spot packaging and an alternate substrate), maximum fulfilment, or a plan that survives the worst case.

ControlFour-eyes, policy-driven

Compliance, finance, planner, procurement and sales approve; nobody approves twice. Execution re-verifies the plan against current data and refuses if it has gone stale.

LoopConfirm, decline, audit

Suppliers confirm or decline. A confirmation becomes a new sourced fact; a decline triggers a re-plan. Every step lands in a hash-chained audit log.

Figures come from the bundled illustrative dataset (run siliconflow demo).
